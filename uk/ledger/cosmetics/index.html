Soap, bath and body

Anything applied to the body to clean, perfume, protect or care for it is a cosmetic. Cosmetics have their own rules, and the safety report comes before the first sale.

Checked 7 November 2026

What the law requires

  • Each product needs a UK Responsible Person, which can be you. They make sure it meets the UK Cosmetics Regulation and keep its Product Information File.

    UK Cosmetics Reg · 1223/2009

  • Each formula needs a Cosmetic Product Safety Report (CPSR) from a qualified safety assessor before it is sold. A new formula or a fragrance change means updating the report.

    UK Cosmetics Reg · 1223/2009

  • Each product must be notified on the government’s Submit Cosmetic Product Notification (SCPN) service before it goes on sale.

    GOV.UK · SCPN

  • Claims must be truthful and backed by evidence. Claiming to treat a condition, such as eczema or anxiety, can make the product a medicine.

    UK Cosmetics Reg · 1223/2009MHRA · borderline products

Northern Ireland and the EU

Selling to Northern Ireland or the EU follows the EU Cosmetics Regulation: a Responsible Person based in Northern Ireland or the EU, and notification on the EU’s CPNP portal. The expanded fragrance allergen list applies to products placed on that market from 31 July 2026.

EU 2023/1545GOV.UK · GPSR in NI

What goes on the label

  • Name and UK address of the Responsible Person
  • Net quantity (weight or volume)
  • Best-before date, or the period-after-opening symbol if it lasts more than 30 months
  • Precautions for use
  • Batch number
  • Function, unless it is obvious from the product
  • Ingredients by INCI name, in descending order
  • Fragrance allergens named when above 0.001% (leave-on) or 0.01% (rinse-off)

Records to keep

  • Product Information File: description, CPSR, manufacturing method and Good Manufacturing Practice statement, evidence for any claims
  • Batch records
  • Supplier documents for every ingredient

Mistakes we see most

  • Selling before the CPSR is signed.
  • Changing the fragrance without updating the CPSR.
  • Using a CPSR written for a different recipe.
  • Wording such as “suitable for eczema” or “heals dry skin”.

Official sources

  • GOV.UK: Regulation 1223/2009 and the Cosmetic Products Enforcement Regulations 2013 (Great Britain)
  • GOV.UK: Submit a cosmetic product notification
  • MHRA: Borderline products: how to tell if your product is a medicine
  • Regulation (EU) 2023/1545 on fragrance allergen labelling
