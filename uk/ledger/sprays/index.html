Pump sprays and aerosols

Room and linen sprays

Sprays usually contain alcohol or solvents, so flammability is the first thing to check. The words on the bottle matter just as much.

Checked 7 November 2026

What the law requires

  • Pump sprays are mixtures. When classified, often as flammable, they need a CLP label.

    GB CLP · 1272/2008HSE · hazard label

  • Aerosols must also meet the Aerosol Dispensers Regulations 2009. Most small makers use a specialist contract filler for them.

    SI 2009/2824

  • Saying a spray kills germs, bacteria or viruses makes it a biocidal product, which has its own approval rules.

    HSE · biocides

What goes on the label

  • Full CLP label where classified, including flammability warnings
  • Your business name, UK address and phone number
  • Net volume
  • Directions, such as “Test on a hidden area of fabric first”
  • Batch number

Records to keep

  • SDS for every ingredient
  • CLP calculation for the finished spray
  • Batch records

Mistakes we see most

  • “Antibacterial”, “kills 99.9% of germs” or “purifies the air” on the label or listing.
  • No flammability warning on an alcohol-based spray.

Official sources

  • HSE: Telling others about the classification: the hazard label
  • Aerosol Dispensers Regulations 2009
  • HSE: biocides (GB Biocidal Products Regulation)
  • GB CLP Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008, as it applies in Great Britain
