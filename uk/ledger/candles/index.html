Candles

What UK law expects before you sell a scented or unscented candle, and the paperwork that shows you did it.

Checked 7 November 2026

What the law requires

  • Your candle must be safe in normal use, and you must give the warnings and information people need to use it safely.

    GPSR 2005 · SI 2005/1803

  • If the finished scented wax is classified as hazardous, usually because of the fragrance, the candle needs a CLP hazard label. Classification depends on your fragrance and your fragrance load, so work it out for each product.

    GB CLP · 1272/2008HSE · hazard label

  • Show your name and address and a product or batch reference, so a product can be traced and recalled if it has to be.

    GPSR 2005 · SI 2005/1803

What goes on the label

  • Product name
  • Your business name, UK address and phone number
  • Net quantity (weight) when a CLP label applies
  • Hazard pictogram(s), signal word, hazard and precautionary statements, where the product is classified
  • The allergen line, for example “Contains linalool. May produce an allergic reaction.”
  • Fire-safety warnings to BS EN 15494, such as “Never leave a burning candle unattended”
  • Batch number

Records to keep

  • Safety data sheet (SDS) for every fragrance oil and dye
  • CLP calculation for each fragrance at the load you use
  • Burn-test results: melt pool, flame height, sooting and container temperature (BS EN 15493 and BS EN 15426 are the reference standards)
  • Batch records: date, wax and fragrance lot numbers, quantities
  • Supplier invoices

Mistakes we see most

  • Copying another maker’s CLP label. It only fits their fragrance at their load.
  • Going above your supplier’s maximum fragrance load for candles (IFRA category 12).
  • Hazard pictograms smaller than 1 cm², or out of proportion to the label.
  • Leaving off the phone number.
