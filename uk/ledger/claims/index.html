6 phrases to look at

Our hand-poured lavender candle is 100% natural and non-toxic. It relieves anxiety, helps you sleep and purifies the air. Made with eco-friendly soy wax.
Check

100% natural · Natural or organic

Fine only if it’s true of the whole product and you can show it. “Organic” needs certified organic ingredients.

Instead: Name the natural ingredient: “scented with essential oils”.

CMA207
Needs evidence

non-toxic · Safety promise

Everything is made of chemicals, and promises of safety need proof you probably don’t have.

Instead: Say what it’s made of: “rapeseed wax, cotton wick and lavender essential oil”.

Remove

relieves anxiety · Health benefit

A promised health effect needs medical evidence that a small maker is unlikely to have.

What we look for

Words to avoid, and what to say instead

Wellbeing wording is fine. Promises about health, germs, safety or the planet need evidence, or a different word.

LevelType of claimSay this instead
RemoveMedicinal claim
Claims to treat, cure or heal can make your product an unlicensed medicine and break the advertising code.		Describe the scent and the feeling: “a soft lavender scent for slow evenings”.
RemoveHealth benefit
A promised health effect needs medical evidence that a small maker is unlikely to have.		“Part of a calm bedtime routine” or “a relaxing scent for the end of the day”.
RemoveMedical condition
Naming a medical condition implies the product helps with it.		Remove the condition. The ASA’s aromatherapy advice accepts general wording such as “promotes a sense of relaxation”.
