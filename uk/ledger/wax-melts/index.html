Wax melts

Wax melts usually carry more fragrance than candles, so most need a full CLP label. Shapes and scent names that look edible are a separate legal risk.

Checked 7 November 2026

What the law requires

  • At the fragrance loads most makers use, wax melts are classified as hazardous and need a CLP label. Check each product against your fragrance’s SDS.

    GB CLP · 1272/2008HSE · hazard label

  • Products that look like food and could be mistaken for it, especially by children, can be banned. Avoid sweet, cake and chocolate shapes, and wording that suggests they can be eaten.

    SI 1989/1291Trading Standards · wax melts

  • Wax melts must be safe and traceable, with your name, address and a batch reference.

    GPSR 2005 · SI 2005/1803

What goes on the label

  • Product name and net weight
  • Your business name, UK address and phone number
  • CLP pictogram(s), signal word, hazard and precautionary statements
  • The allergen line where required
  • Use warnings, such as “Never leave a lit burner unattended” and “Keep out of reach of children and pets”
  • Batch number

Records to keep

  • SDS and CLP calculation for every fragrance and load
  • Batch records
  • A photo of each label version, with the date you started using it

Mistakes we see most

  • Giving away samples without labels. Free samples still need them.
  • Pictograms squeezed onto clamshell edges where nobody can read them.
  • Food-like shapes, or wording such as “good enough to eat”.

Official sources

  • Lincolnshire Trading Standards: advice to wax melt sellers
  • Food Imitations (Safety) Regulations 1989
  • HSE: Telling others about the classification: the hazard label
  • GB CLP Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008, as it applies in Great Britain
